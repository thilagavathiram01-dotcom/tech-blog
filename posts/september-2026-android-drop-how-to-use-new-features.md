# How to Use the September 2026 Android Drop Features

Google’s monthly Android Drop is not a full operating-system upgrade. It is a smaller package of features that roll out through Pixel Feature Drops and Google apps. The September 2026 Drop is a good example: five practical tools that help you find things, stay comfortable in a moving car, see more of the world with Gemini, and collaborate inside Google Messages.

This guide explains what each feature does, who can use it, and how to try it without guessing.

## What shipped in the September 2026 Android Drop

Google announced the Drop on September 1, 2026. The five features are:

- Find Hub remembered items, stored with help from Gemini
- Motion Assist, a passenger-seat overlay that can reduce motion sickness
- Guided vision in Gemini Live
- Google Keep notes and lists inside Google Messages
- Custom Chat themes in Google Messages

Availability is not the same for every phone. Some features need Android 16 or Android 17. Others depend on Gemini being available in your country. Treat the steps below as the official intended workflow; if a toggle is missing, the feature has not reached your device yet.

## 1. Ask Gemini to remember where you put something

Tracker tags are useful for keys and bags. They are less useful for a passport, a spare house key, or a document you hide in a drawer. Find Hub remembered items is meant for those cases.

Google says you will be able to tell Gemini where you placed an item. Gemini saves that location in Find Hub. You can attach an optional photo. Later, you ask Gemini for the location or open the remembered items tab in Find Hub.

**Try this:**

1. Use an Android 16 or later phone in a country where both Gemini and Find Hub are supported.
2. Say something like: “Hey Google, remember in Find Hub that I put my passport in my bedroom drawer.”
3. Add a photo if Gemini offers the option.
4. When you need the item, ask Gemini where it is, or open Find Hub and check remembered items.

Use this for items you will not tag. Do not treat it as a live GPS location. It stores what you told Gemini, not a constantly updating map pin.

## 2. Turn on Motion Assist in the passenger seat

Motion Assist is for people who get queasy while reading or watching video in a moving vehicle. It places a subtle bubble overlay on the screen that moves with the vehicle. Google’s idea is to give your eyes a visual cue that matches the motion your inner ear already feels.

Google says you can set it to turn on automatically, pin it in Quick Settings, and change the bubble’s shape, color, and opacity.

**Try this:**

1. Use a phone running Android 17.
2. Open Settings and search for Motion Assist, or look in Quick Settings after the update arrives.
3. Enable automatic activation if you ride as a passenger often.
4. Adjust opacity so the overlay is visible without covering text.
5. Test it on a short trip before a long one.

This is a passenger feature. Do not use your phone as a driver, overlay or not.

## 3. Use Guided vision in Gemini Live

Guided vision lets you share your camera in Gemini Live and hear descriptions of what is in front of you. Google built it with the blind and low-vision communities. Typical uses include reading fine print on a food label, checking a dim restaurant menu, or identifying a household object.

If the camera is not lined up, Gemini can tell you to reframe, pan, or center the object. You can add Guided vision to Accessibility shortcuts, enable it from the TalkBack menu, or turn it on in Gemini app settings.

Google says Guided vision is coming to phones running Android 9 or later in countries where Gemini is available.

**Try this:**

1. Open the Gemini app and start Gemini Live.
2. Share the camera.
3. Point at a label, menu, or object and ask what it shows.
4. Follow the voice prompts if Gemini asks you to reframe.
5. For faster access, add it to Accessibility shortcuts.

Google’s own footnote is important: Guided vision can make mistakes. It is not a medical device, mobility aid, or navigation tool. Do not use it for obstacle detection or wayfinding.

## 4. Share a Google Keep list inside Google Messages

This is the most immediately useful feature for families and group trips. You can create or attach a Google Keep note or list from inside a Messages chat. Everyone in the thread can view, edit, and add items without leaving the conversation.

Google says rollout started on September 1, 2026 in Google Messages.

**Try this:**

1. Open Google Messages and go into a chat.
2. Tap the + icon.
3. Choose Google Keep.
4. Create a new list or attach an existing note.
5. Let the group edit groceries, packing items, or trip ideas in place.

If you already keep shared lists in Keep, this removes the extra step of switching apps just to add milk.

## 5. Give each chat its own theme

Chat theme lets you set a distinct background and color treatment for each conversation. You can pick a wallpaper or use your own photo. Text bubbles can match the theme, or you can choose colors separately.

Google’s examples are simple: a coffee-colored theme for one friend, a beach photo for a vacation group. The feature is available now in Google Messages, according to the September announcement.

**Try this:**

1. Open a conversation in Google Messages.
2. Open that chat’s options or theme settings.
3. Choose a wallpaper, photo, or color set.
4. Check that bubble colors stay readable on the new background.

Themes are cosmetic. They do not change delivery, RCS status, or privacy settings.

## What you need before the features appear

| Feature | What Google says you need |
| --- | --- |
| Find Hub remembered items | Android 16+, Gemini and Find Hub supported in your country |
| Motion Assist | Android 17 |
| Guided vision | Android 9+, Gemini available; rolling out |
| Keep in Messages | Google Messages; rollout started September 1, 2026 |
| Chat themes | Google Messages; available now |

Update Google, Gemini, Messages, and Find Hub from Play Store. Pixel Feature Drops and monthly Google app updates often land over several days, not all at once.

## Practical ways to combine the features

A weekend trip is a good test:

- Store the spare Airbnb key location in Find Hub before you leave.
- Share a Keep packing list in the group Messages thread.
- Theme that vacation chat so it is easy to find.
- Use Motion Assist if you are the passenger on the drive.
- Use Guided vision only for labels or objects, not for walking directions.

None of these replace common sense. Remembered items are only as accurate as the last thing you told Gemini. Guided vision can misread text. Motion Assist will not fix every case of motion sickness.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 Android Drop is a small update with a clear theme: reduce friction in daily tasks. Find Hub remembered items covers things you will not tag. Motion Assist is a passenger comfort tool. Guided vision adds camera help in Gemini Live, with explicit limits. Keep-in-Messages and chat themes make group chats easier to use and easier to recognize.

If only Messages features have arrived on your phone, start there. They do not require a new Android version. Check again after your next system and app updates for Find Hub, Motion Assist, and Guided vision.
