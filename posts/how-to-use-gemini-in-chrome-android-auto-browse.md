# How to Use Gemini in Chrome on Android (Including Auto Browse)

Reading a long article on a phone usually means pinch-zooming, hopping between tabs, and copying text into another app. Gemini in Chrome is meant to cut that loop. The assistant sits inside Chrome, can see the page you are on, and can send useful bits to Calendar, Keep, or Gmail without you leaving the browser.

On paid Google AI plans in the United States, it can also run auto browse: multi-step web tasks such as comparing products or starting a reservation, with you confirming sensitive steps.

This guide covers what Google has actually shipped, who can use it, how to open it, and how to run an auto browse task without handing the agent a blank check.

## What Gemini in Chrome is on Android

Gemini in Chrome is part of the Chrome app. It is not the same as opening gemini.google.com in a tab. Google’s product pages note that the in-browser assistant can share page content; a regular Gemini tab cannot do that in the same way.

On Android it is designed to:

- Answer questions about the current page
- Summarize long articles
- Explain dense sections without switching apps
- Connect to Google apps such as Calendar, Keep, and Gmail
- Generate or customize images with Nano Banana from a page you are viewing
- Run auto browse tasks if you are on Google AI Pro or Ultra

Google announced the Android experience in May 2026 and said the broader U.S. rollout for Gemini in Chrome reached all Android users there on August 18, 2026. Auto browse stayed a Pro and Ultra feature.

## What you need before you start

Requirements come from Google’s Chrome Help article and the May 2026 launch post.

**Gemini in Chrome (page help and summaries)**

- Android 12 or higher
- At least 4 GB of RAM
- Latest Chrome
- Signed in to Chrome with a Google Account
- Device language set to English (U.S.) at launch
- Available to personal accounts in the U.S. as of the August 18, 2026 blog post

**Auto browse**

- Age 18 or over, in the U.S.
- Device language set to English
- Google AI Pro or Google AI Ultra on a personal account
- Opt in to Gemini in Chrome and grant permission for Chrome to browse on your behalf
- Safe Browsing set to Standard or Enhanced
- Not Incognito
- Work or school accounts only if an admin enables it
- Not available in Gemini Live chats or on iPhone/iPad at the time Google published the help page

If the Gemini icon is missing, update Chrome, confirm language and region, and check **Chrome > Settings > Gemini in Chrome**.

## How to open Gemini in Chrome

Google’s August 2026 Android post is specific:

1. Open Chrome on your phone.
2. Go to any page.
3. Tap the Gemini icon in the top bar, or open the three-dot menu and choose Gemini.

A sheet slides up from the bottom so you can type while the page stays visible. You can dock that sheet and keep scrolling.

Useful first prompts on a page you already opened:

- “Summarize this article in five bullets.”
- “What does this section assume I already know?”
- “Extract the dates, prices, and cancellation rules.”
- “Add the event details on this page to Calendar.”
- “Put the ingredient list into a Keep note.”

Google’s May post uses the same pattern: page context first, then a connected-app action.

### Images with Nano Banana

Inside the same panel you can ask Gemini to turn the page into a study graphic or to alter an image you are looking at (Google’s example: add furniture to an apartment listing photo). Treat those as drafts. Do not use generated images as evidence that a listing, product, or document is accurate.

## How to run an auto browse task

Official Chrome Help steps:

1. Open Chrome on Android.
2. Tap **Ask Gemini**.
3. Describe the task in the text box.
4. Tap **Submit**.
5. Read the plan. Check that it understood the prompt, the page, and any personal details it intends to use.
6. If the plan is right, tap **Start Task**.

While it works, the tab shows an auto browse icon. You can leave Chrome. When the job finishes, Chrome can send a notification if notifications are on.

**Controls while a task is running**

- **Switch tabs** next to the task in chat to jump to the working tab.
- **Check your task** in the top bar if Gemini needs you.
- **Take over task** on the task tab when you want to click yourself.
- **Pause**, **Resume**, or **Stop** in the Gemini panel. Closing the task tab also stops it.

Google’s own examples include booking parking from ticket details, updating a recurring pet-food order, finding travel stays, comparing products, drafting calendar items, and fetching receipts. Use tasks you would be willing to undo.

### Daily limits Google publishes

- Google AI Pro: up to 20 multi-step requests per day
- Google AI Ultra: up to 200 multi-step requests per day

There is also a cap on how many multi-step tasks can run at once. Chrome tells you when you hit the limit.

## Safety rules worth treating as part of the workflow

Auto browse is experimental. Google’s help page is blunt: you are responsible for what the agent does, including mistakes and unexpected purchases.

Read the plan before **Start Task**. Gemini chooses which sites to visit and may share personal information with those sites, including data from Connected Apps. It can use sites you are already signed into. Google Password Manager can help it sign in if you allow that; it does not give Gemini the raw password. You can revoke site sign-in later under **Settings > Gemini in Chrome > Sites Gemini can sign you in to**.

Google says the agent should ask for confirmation before sensitive steps such as purchases, posting, sending messages, submitting forms, or touching highly sensitive financial or health sites. It may ask you to take over for payments, terms of service, or account creation.

Still watch the tab when money, medical, legal, or account-recovery flows are involved. Prompt injection is a documented risk: a page can contain instructions aimed at the agent rather than at you. Stop the task if the plan drifts.

Turn auto browse off anytime: **Chrome > Settings > Gemini in Chrome > Let Gemini browse for you**.

## A 10-minute practice run

Use a low-stakes task first.

1. Open a public product-comparison article in Chrome.
2. Ask Gemini to summarize the page and list three differences that matter for a budget buyer.
3. If you have Pro or Ultra, ask auto browse to find current prices for one model and add the cheapest in-stock option to a cart — then stop before checkout.
4. Confirm it paused for purchase confirmation.
5. Tap **Stop** or **Take over task**.

If those steps work, the feature is doing what Google advertised: page-aware help for everyone in the U.S. rollout, and supervised web chores for paid plans.

## Conclusion

Gemini in Chrome on Android is useful when the page is already open and you want a summary, an explanation, or a note in Keep without leaving Chrome. Auto browse is a separate, paid, U.S.-gated agent. Treat it as a supervised intern: give a narrow task, read the plan, keep notifications on, and take over before money moves.

If the icon is not there yet, you are likely outside the current region, language, RAM, or account rules Google published. Update Chrome and check the Gemini in Chrome settings rather than hunting for a hidden flag.
