# How to Use App Bubbles on Android 17

Android 17 made floating apps a system feature instead of a messaging trick. You can long-press almost any app and open it as a Bubble: a compact window that stays on top, then shrinks to an icon when you leave it.

If you already used chat-head style bubbles in Messages, this is the same idea with a much wider job. Maps, notes, a podcast player, a calculator, or a group chat can sit at the edge of the screen while you stay in another app.

This guide covers how to create bubbles, how the Bubble Bar works on large screens, and when the feature is worth using.

## What App Bubbles actually do

Google describes App Bubbles as a way to turn any app into a floating window from the launcher. On phones, the window can take most of the display, then collapse to a movable icon. On foldables and tablets, open bubbles sit in a dedicated Bubble Bar so they stay organized instead of covering the whole workspace.

A few limits matter:

- You can keep several apps bubbled at once. Reporting around the Pixel launch commonly cites a stack of up to five.
- Games and some full-screen apps may not behave well in a tiny window.
- The feature arrived first on supported Pixel devices with the Android 17 stable release in June 2026. Other manufacturers are shipping it with their Android 17 skins throughout 2026.

Bubbles are not split screen. Split screen still divides the display into two apps of similar size. A bubble is for a secondary app you want nearby, not for two apps that need equal space.

## Check that you have Android 17

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **About phone** (or **About tablet**).
3. Confirm the Android version is 17.
4. If an update is waiting, go to **System** → **System update** and install it.

If your phone is still on Android 16, you will not see a Bubble action on long-press. Wait for your manufacturer’s Android 17 rollout rather than hunting for a hidden toggle.

## How to open an app as a Bubble

The official path is from the launcher, not from inside the app.

1. Go to the home screen or open the app drawer.
2. Long-press the app you want to keep nearby.
3. Tap **Bubble** in the shortcut menu.
4. The app opens in a compact floating window.
5. Tap outside the window, or tap the bubble icon, to shrink it to the edge of the screen.
6. Tap the icon again when you need the app.

Repeat for other apps you use all day. As you add more, Android groups them. On phones, that group is a stack of icons. On large screens, they move into the Bubble Bar.

If the Bubble action is missing:

- Confirm the phone is on Android 17.
- Try a different launcher app, such as the default Pixel Launcher.
- Update the system and Google Play system updates, then reboot.

Some third-party launchers may not expose the new shortcut immediately.

## How to use the Bubble Bar on foldables and tablets

On large screens (typically foldables and tablets), Android 17 adds a Bubble Bar. Google places it as part of the system taskbar so floating apps have a dock instead of floating loose on the canvas.

Typical use:

1. Unfold the device or use a tablet in landscape.
2. Bubble one or more apps from the launcher.
3. Look for the Bubble Bar on the taskbar, often toward the bottom or bottom-right.
4. Tap a bubble to expand it.
5. Switch between bubbled apps from the bar instead of hunting icons on the wallpaper.
6. Dismiss a bubble when you are done so the bar stays short.

This is the most useful form of the feature. On a foldable, you can keep a notes app or a maps route docked while a document or video fills the rest of the screen.

## Everyday ways to use Bubbles

Bubbles work best when the second app is something you glance at, not something you live in.

**Messaging without leaving work.** Bubble Messages or another chat app while you stay in Gmail, Docs, or a browser tab. Reply, then collapse the chat.

**Directions while you pack or shop.** Bubble Maps. Keep the route visible while you check a list in another app. Do this only as a passenger or while standing still.

**Notes next to a recipe or ticket.** Bubble Keep or a notes app. Copy an address, a confirmation number, or a shopping quantity without flipping back and forth.

**Media controls.** Bubble a podcast or music app if you change tracks often and do not want to open the full player.

**Calculator or converter.** Bubble it over a spreadsheet or shopping page, then dismiss it.

Avoid bubbling the same app you already have in split screen. You will fight two window systems at once.

## How to dismiss or clean up bubbles

A leftover bubble is just visual noise.

- Drag a bubble toward the bottom of the screen if you see a dismiss target, the same way older chat heads worked.
- Open the bubble stack or Bubble Bar and remove the app you no longer need.
- Close the floating window from the app’s own controls if the bubble only minimized the activity.

If a bubbled app feels stuck, force-stop is rarely needed. Opening the full app from the launcher usually returns it to a normal task.

## What developers had to change

You do not need this section to use the feature, but it explains why some apps look cramped.

Android 17 treats adaptive windows as the default on large screens. Apps that target API 37 can no longer lock orientation or refuse resizing on displays wider than 600 dp, with games as a notable exception. Bubbles, the Bubble Bar, and interactive desktop Picture-in-Picture all assume an app can survive a small or moving window.

If an app redraws badly, that is usually the app, not a broken bubble. Update the app from Play Store before you decide the feature is unusable.

## App Bubbles vs split screen vs Picture-in-Picture

Use the window type that matches the job:

| Goal | Better tool |
| --- | --- |
| Two apps that both need a large workspace | Split screen |
| A video that should stay on top while you browse | Picture-in-Picture |
| A utility or chat you open for 10 seconds at a time | App Bubble |
| Moving a task from phone to tablet | Android 17 Continue On, where available |

On desktop-style Android sessions, Google also added interactive PiP: pinned windows stay usable instead of becoming a read-only preview. That is separate from Bubbles, but it is part of the same multitasking push.

## Troubleshooting

**No Bubble option on long-press.** You are not on Android 17, or the launcher does not support the shortcut yet.

**The window is tiny and unusable.** Expand it, or stop bubbling that app. Use split screen instead.

**The bubble covers controls.** Drag it to the opposite edge. On a tablet, prefer the Bubble Bar.

**Battery feels worse.** Each bubbled app is still running. Keep only the two or three you actually reopen.

**A work profile app will not bubble.** Some managed profiles restrict overlay-style windows. Check with the device admin policy.

## Conclusion

App Bubbles are the most visible everyday change in Android 17. They are not a new operating system inside the operating system. They are a faster way to keep a second app one tap away.

Start with one app you already open constantly: chat, notes, or maps. If that saves you from task-switching, add a second. If the stack gets in the way, drop back to split screen. The feature is useful because it is optional.

On a foldable or tablet, give the Bubble Bar a day of real use. That is where Android 17’s version of floating apps stops feeling like a chat-head leftover and starts feeling like a small dock for work.
